# 7 Powers — Hamilton Helmer

## Core Idea

Strategy is a route to **continuing Power in significant markets**.

**Power** is the ability to sustain superior economic returns despite competition.

**Business Value = Market Scale × Power**

Every Power requires two components:
- **Benefit:** Higher prices, lower costs, or reduced investment.
- **Barrier:** Something preventing competitors from copying or neutralizing the benefit.

**Key principle:** Always look for the Barrier first. Operational excellence is necessary but not sufficient for lasting competitive advantage.

## The 7 Powers

### 1. Scale Economies
Unit costs decrease as production volume increases.

- **Benefit:** Lower costs.
- **Barrier:** Competitors face prohibitive costs to gain market share.
- **Example:** Netflix spreads fixed content costs across millions of subscribers.
- **Insight:** Scale alone isn't enough; you need a meaningful scale advantage over competitors.

### 2. Network Economies
The product becomes more valuable as more people use it.

- **Benefit:** Higher customer value and pricing power.
- **Barrier:** Competitors cannot easily overcome the established network.
- **Example:** LinkedIn's professional network.
- **Insight:** Early market leadership can create winner-take-all dynamics. Network effects must be strong within the relevant network.

### 3. Counter-Positioning
A newcomer introduces a superior business model that incumbents hesitate to copy.

- **Benefit:** Superior economics or customer value.
- **Barrier:** Incumbents risk damaging their existing businesses.
- **Example:** Vanguard's low-cost index funds versus traditional active managers.
- **Insight:** An incumbent's greatest strength can become its weakness. This Power protects against incumbents, not necessarily other newcomers.

### 4. Switching Costs
Customers incur costs or lose value when changing providers.

- **Benefit:** Greater retention and pricing power on future purchases.
- **Barrier:** Competitors must compensate customers for switching costs.
- **Example:** SAP enterprise software.
- **Types:**
  - Financial: Replacement and migration costs.
  - Procedural: Retraining, disruption, and operational risks.
  - Relational: Loss of established relationships.
- **Insight:** Switching costs create the most value when customers make recurring or complementary purchases.

### 5. Branding
Customers assign greater value to a product because of its established reputation.

- **Benefit:** Higher willingness to pay.
- **Barrier:** Competitors cannot rapidly recreate accumulated trust and associations.
- **Example:** Tiffany & Co.
- **Sources:**
  - Affective valence: Positive emotions and identity.
  - Uncertainty reduction: Trust in consistent quality.
- **Insight:** A strong brand is built through consistent reinforcement over time, not advertising alone.

### 6. Cornered Resource
Exclusive or preferential access to a valuable, scarce asset.

- **Benefit:** Superior products or lower costs.
- **Barrier:** Competitors cannot access an equivalent resource on comparable terms.
- **Example:** Pixar's original creative Brain Trust.
- **Insight:** The resource must be valuable, scarce, and defensibly controlled.

### 7. Process Power
Superior organizational processes that competitors struggle to replicate.

- **Benefit:** Better quality or lower costs.
- **Barrier:** Complex, deeply embedded processes require years to reproduce.
- **Example:** Toyota Production System.
- **Insight:** Operational excellence becomes Power only when the underlying processes are difficult to imitate.

## Strategy Dynamics

### The Path to Power

**Invention is the starting point of Power.**

- Create something genuinely valuable.
- Establish a defensible competitive advantage.
- Scale and reinforce that advantage.
- Adapt strategy as the market evolves.

A great product alone is insufficient. It must lead to a lasting Benefit protected by a Barrier.

### The Power Progression

Different Powers are best established at different stages.

| Stage | Powers |
|---|---|
| Origination | Counter-Positioning, Cornered Resource |
| Takeoff | Scale Economies, Network Economies, Switching Costs |
| Stability | Branding, Process Power |

**Key insight:** Strategic opportunities have windows. Some advantages become extremely difficult to establish once the market matures.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Power > execution:** Execution realizes value; Power makes superior returns sustainable.
2. **Barriers matter most:** Benefits without barriers get competed away.
3. **Strategy is relative:** Evaluate advantages against specific competitors.
4. **Invention creates opportunity:** Differentiation is often the first step toward Power.
5. **Timing matters:** Establish advantages while the relevant window is open.
6. **Multiple Powers compound:** The strongest businesses can combine different advantages.
7. **Persistence creates value:** Long-term defensibility matters more than short-term profitability.
